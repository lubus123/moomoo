## 1. The headline

The US is in the middle of the largest dairy-processing build-out in its modern history: analysts put the total at **~$8–11 billion across 50+ new or expanded plants, mostly cheese- and whey-led, coming online 2025–2028** (CoBank $8B through 2027; Rabobank >$7B 2023–26; broader trade-press tallies ~$10–11B / 53 plants).

The single biggest step-up landed in **2025**, when four anchor cheese/butter plants reached operation more or less at once:

- **Leprino Foods — Lubbock, TX** (~$1B, ~8M lbs milk/day, mozzarella + whey)
- **Hilmar Cheese — Dodge City, KS** (>$600M, ~13M lbs/day *estimated*, American cheese + whey)
- **Valley Queen — Milbank, SD** (+3M lbs/day, cheese + WPC)
- **Great Lakes Cheese — Franklinville, NY** (~$518M+, replacing the old Cuba NY plant)

plus **Darigold Pasco, WA** (butter/powder, ~8M lbs/day) and **fairlife Webster, NY** (UF milk, ~5M lbs/day).

A second wave (whey-protein plants, Walmart Robinson TX, Idaho Milk Products) fills in **2026**, and a third wave — **Southwest Cheese WPI (2027), Land O'Lakes Tulare (2027), Bel/Babybel Brookings (2028), fairlife Coopersville (2028), Chobani Rome NY (2028), Daisy Brand Boone IA (2028/29)** — extends the ramp out to the end of the decade.

**Rough incremental milk draw** from the projects with disclosed intake is on the order of **25–30 million lbs of milk/day (~10–11 billion lbs/yr)** at full run — before counting the many projects that never disclosed a lbs/day figure.

## 2. Where it's happening — the map is moving

Capacity and cows are shifting decisively to the **Central Plains and Southwest** (TX, KS, NM, SD / the I-29 corridor) and away from traditional Upper Midwest and California:

- Texas milk output was up **+10.6% YoY** (April 2025), >50% of national herd expansion; Amarillo dispatching 1,100+ semi-loads/day.
- Herd additions cluster where the plants are: **TX +46k cows, KS +29k, SD +21k, NY +8k** (AFB).
- Terrain: CO/KS/TX/SD together produced **~5M lb more milk/day** (Dec 2024 vs 2023) while national output *fell* ~3M lb/day.
- Offsetting: **Leprino Lemoore East (CA)** closing early 2026; Wisconsin has lost ~76% of its dairy farms since the mid-2010s.

## 3. What the analysts are saying (by theme)

**Is there enough milk?** This is the central tension. US milk production fell two years running (2023, 2024) for the first time since the 1960s, and the replacement-heifer inventory is at a 20-year low. CoBank sees the heifer shortage bottoming in 2025–26 with a herd rebound only from **2027**. In the meantime, plants are being fed by **higher components** (milkfat 4.33%, +2.1% in 2025), retained older cows, and HPAI recovery — not new headcount.

**Price impact.** USDA (Feb 2026) pins lower 2026 prices explicitly on *both* more milk *and* more processing capacity, "particularly for cheese":
- All-milk **$18.95/cwt** (2026F) vs $21.17 (2025)
- Class III **$16.65**, Class IV **$15.70**
- Cheddar $1.60/lb, Butter $1.68/lb (lowest since 2020)
- "Higher whey prices will not offset the effect of lower cheese prices."

**The whey surprise.** The pre-boom fear was a whey glut; the reality is a **bifurcated market**. Commodity dry whey is soft (~$0.55/lb), but **high-protein WPC80/WPI are in genuine shortage** — WPC80 ran from ~$5.80/lb (Oct 2025) to ~$10.50/lb (Q1 2026), a ~19x premium over dry whey. The binding constraint is **fractionation/spray-dry capacity, not cheese vats**, and new fractionation capacity "won't produce until 2027–2028." Demand driver: the GLP-1 / high-protein consumer trend. (StoneX, Vesper, USDA)

**Distressed milk / basis.** Showing up regionally in the **Upper Midwest** (milk dumping, negative PPDs, depooling, spot loads trading $3 under to $3 over Class), while the milk-short **Plains/Southwest** keep pulling cows in. The June 2025 FMMO make-allowance increase mechanically lowered classified milk values and raised basis risk for hedgers.

**The risk case.** Bullvine/Ever.Ag/Daily Dairy Report frame a **12–24 month surplus window** before demand catches up, with inefficient plants forced to run below capacity or close unless exports grow. Mike North (Ever.Ag): *"We lack sufficient animals for all plants."*

**Exports as the pressure valve.** 2025 delivered record milkfat exports (+40% YoY; butter +152%). But StoneX warns four of five major exporters are growing milk at once and competition is intense into 2026 — a crowded valve, and US dairy is heavily reliant on Mexico (~28% of exports).

## 4. Consensus bottom line

- **Magnitude:** ~$8–11B / 50+ plants, cheese-and-whey-led, mostly 2025–2027.
- **Near term (2025–26):** capacity ran *ahead* of milk headcount, but components + retained cows kept vats fed → lower cheese/butter and lower Class III/IV.
- **The real story is whey:** commodity dry whey soft; WPC80/WPI in acute shortage (fractionation is the bottleneck).
- **Tightening is a 2027 story** as the heifer-driven herd rebuild finally arrives.

## Data-quality notes / caveats

- **Hilmar Dodge City** ~13M lbs/day is *estimated from ~260 trucks/day*; no official lbs/day published.
- **Great Lakes Cheese** local "4M gal/day" claim conflicts with its 1.42B lbs/yr figure and is likely overstated; net incremental over the retired Cuba plant is ~2M lbs/day.
- **California Dairies (VNB)** lbs/day are gallon→lb conversions (8.6 lb/gal); investment undisclosed.
- **Agropur** 2025–26 spend is a value-added modernization, not a big milk-intake jump.
- **DFA (Monroe WI), Actus (Sparta WI)** are acquisitions of existing capacity, not new milk intake.
- **"Great Plains Processing / Great Prairie"** (in the original brief) could not be confirmed as a dairy project — the $500M Mitchell, SD plant under construction is grain, not dairy.
- **fairlife Arizona/Peoria** — no new upcoming plant; Goodyear AZ opened 2021 (background only).
- **Bubs** has no confirmed US manufacturing plant.
- **Perrigo** ~$240M formula investment is under strategic review (Nov 2025).
- A few aggregate figures ("$11B / 53 plants," "~50% utilization") come from trade-press aggregation / search snippets of Dairy Herd & Hoard's articles that could not be fetched directly.
